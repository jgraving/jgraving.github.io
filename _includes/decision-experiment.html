Every run starts 50/50 between A and B. Small internal fluctuations break the symmetry.

What am I seeing?

Each path starts with equal internal weights for A and B. Movement follows their weighted bearings. As the agent moves, the angle between those bearings changes: aligned options support equal weighting, while sufficiently separated options compete. Small, zero-mean internal fluctuations can then grow into commitment.

This demonstration uses coupled belief and movement updates, with a 90-degree cooperation threshold. The coupling strength, update rates, and noise level are illustrative choices, not estimates from animal data. Motor noise is switched off here so the effect of internal noise is easier to see. Equal internal weights need not mean equal final choice frequencies when you move the targets into an asymmetric arrangement.
